# The assistant (AAVE)

*The Assistant Audiovisual Engineer, defined almost entirely by reference to the lead. Source: `URITP Assistant Audiovisual Engineer`, version 2026; checked 2026-07-30.*

The Assistant Audiovisual Engineer aids the AVE in completing everything a production requires, and assists with routine maintenance, use and inventory of the department's audio equipment.

The reporting line is the thing to understand first: **you report to the Production Manager through the Audiovisual Engineer.** Not directly. That one word is the whole shape of the job — your channel is the AVE, and going around them is how a production ends up with two answers to one question.

## Enrollment and attendance

A two-credit THTR production course, rather than the full technical theatre sequence the lead takes. The source names it as a placeholder rather than a course number, so confirm the actual course before telling anyone to register.

You attend weekly production meetings, the meet and greet, and postmortems. You set weekly office hours, ideally overlapping the AVE or sound lab. You complete the required safety trainings. And you provide AV support for other department events like the One Acts Festival and UR Performing, attendance negotiable.

## Where you have to be

You are present to **work and lead** during all AV work sessions: hang, focus, sound check, strike. Note the difference from the lead, who supervises — you are expected to have your hands on the gear.

You assist in confirming and training board ops, and you work closely with Stage Management on backstage needs during the show. You help train and document the AV crew on safe use of the equipment, and sound lab attendance is typically required to fulfill that.

## What you actually do on a production

Inventory reviews alongside the AVE. Reviewing all sound plots, patches and paperwork **with** them, which is the clause that makes this an apprenticeship rather than an errand list. Helping plan the AV load-in and strike. Attending additional work sessions on request, including designer run, tech and dress. Handling sound crew and sound-note documentation and communication.

That last one is the job inside the job. Notes and comms are where an assistant becomes indispensable, because the lead cannot be on headset and in the rack at the same time.

---

*One thing to know about the source: its deliverables section is a verbatim copy of the lead's — same production archive, same feasibility pass, same "maintains and updates the AVE Handbook." Read literally, the assistant owns every deliverable the AVE owns, which contradicts the rest of the document. In practice the deliverables are the lead's and the assistant contributes to them; the real division has never been written down.*
