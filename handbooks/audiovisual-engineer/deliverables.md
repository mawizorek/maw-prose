# Deliverables

*What you owe, and roughly when. Source: `URITP Audiovisual Engineer`, Duties and Responsibilities, version 2026. Content last checked: 2026-07-30.*

## Going in

You provide the **Sound and Video Designers** the paperwork they need to design against: plots, budget, inventory, and tech specs. Then you go the other direction and track and review everything that comes back from them with the Production Manager, the Technical Director, and the other department heads.

**Before the process starts, the AV inventory is accurate and up to date.** Not roughly. A designer's plot built against a wrong count is a conversation you will have twice.

## The feasibility pass, and the part that is uniquely yours

On every plot version you provide a **feasibility update**. Then two things that no designer submits and you have to author: the **install plan** and the **cabling plan**.

And one that is specific to this role and easy to skip: **you reference other departments' plans against AV needs.** Lighting positions, scenic elements, and projection surfaces compete for the same air and the same sightlines. Nobody else in the building is checking that a speaker position survives the scenic build, so if you do not do it, it does not happen until load-in, when it is expensive.

## Coming out

A **production archive** for each production, curated and submitted by you:

- the final plots, **as hung**, not as drawn
- show files and media
- the patch list
- run sheets
- network and device arrangement, tech notes, shared drives

**As-hung is the whole point of the archive.** A designer's file records what somebody wanted; yours records what was actually in the air and patched, which is the only version worth inheriting.

**After every production**, the AV inventory gets updated again. Beginning and end, every time.

And you maintain and update the AVE Handbook, which is what this package is. **If something in here is wrong, that is now your job.**
