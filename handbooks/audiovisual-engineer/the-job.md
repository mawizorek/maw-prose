# The job

*What the Audiovisual Engineer is on the hook for. Source: `URITP Audiovisual Engineer`, version 2026; checked 2026-07-30.*

You are the contact person for every audio, video and projection question on your production, and the custodian of the AV inventory and its maintenance and storage for the length of your assignment. Note that the second half includes new equipment — gear that arrives on your watch becomes your inventory problem.

## Who you answer to, and who answers to you

You report to the **Production Manager**. You work closely with both the **Sound Designer and the Video Designer** — two designers, not one, and they will not necessarily be talking to each other — and with the **Technical Director**.

Below you: the **Assistant Audiovisual Engineer** or engineers, whom you mentor and supervise, and whose call schedule you define explicitly. You confirm the AV show-run crew positions with Production Management and train the **board operators**. And you work closely with **Stage Management to assess backstage needs during the show**, which is the relationship that decides whether comms and monitoring actually work when it counts.

You help train and document the AV crew in safe use of everything they touch: ladders, scaffolds, amps, speakers, mics, cameras, projectors, monitors, consoles, tools, cables. Attendance at sound lab is typically required to fulfill that — read it as what it is: the training obligation has a scheduled home, and skipping the lab means it goes unmet rather than absorbed elsewhere.

## What you have to be enrolled in and show up to

One of: Technical Theatre (THTR121), Advanced Technical Theatre (THTR221), or an internship or independent study (ENG 391 or 394).

Attendance covers weekly production meetings, the meet and greet, and postmortems. You set and hold weekly office hours, ideally overlapping with the assistant or with sound lab — the overlap is the point, not the hours. You complete the required safety trainings.

And you provide AV support for other department events like the One Acts Festival and UR Performing events. Attendance there is negotiable, which is the only negotiable item in the whole role, so treat it as the one lever you have.

## Where you have to physically be

You lead and supervise all AV work sessions in person: **hang, focus, sound check, strike.**

Day to day through the whole production process, the modifications, troubleshooting and repair of AV requirements and equipment are yours.

## The systems that are yours and nobody thinks about until they fail

**Intercom, backstage monitoring, and the paging system.** You set them and you maintain them for the run. Nobody notices them working and everybody notices them broken, and they are usually the last thing set and the first thing that gets bumped.

You also provide AV or video equipment for the rehearsal process on request and as available, and support **archival video recording** as needed. That second one is easy to lose track of, because it is the only deliverable in the job with an audience that is not in the building.

And you request purchases and rentals for anything the show needs that the inventory does not have, sometimes including the pickup and the return.

## Strike

A safe strike of all AV equipment, with the theatre and your assigned storage areas left clean and organized. The condition you leave storage in is the condition the next AVE inherits.
