# The job

*What the Head Electrician is on the hook for. Source: `URITP Head Electrician`, version 2026; checked 2026-07-30.*

You are the contact person for every lighting and electrical question on your production, and the custodian of the lighting instruments and equipment for the length of your assignment. Both halves matter. The first is what people ask you for; the second is what you get blamed for in November when nobody can find the cable.

## Who you answer to, and who answers to you

You report to the **Production Manager**. You work closely with the production **Lighting Designer** and the **Technical Director**, and you are the lead on all communication with the LD about production needs. Take that last part literally: if the LD is telling three different people three different things, that is your problem to collapse into one channel.

Below you: the **Assistant Head Electrician** or electricians, whom you mentor and supervise, and whose call schedule you define explicitly. Vague calls are the fastest way to lose an assistant. You send crew calls to your assigned electrics crew, and you train the **board operators**. Stage management is a peer you will be talking to constantly, especially about anything that touches the deck.

You also help train and document the electrics crew in safe use of everything they touch: ladders, scaffolds, circuits, dimmers, consoles, tools, instruments, and cable.

## What you have to be enrolled in and show up to

One of these, for credit: Technical Theatre (THTR121), Advanced Technical Theatre (THTR221), or an internship or independent study (ENG 391 or 394).

Attendance is not optional on weekly production meetings, the meet and greet, postmortems, and **Friday lighting labs for both shows in the semester**, not just yours. You set and hold weekly office hours, a minimum of two hours a week for production work, and you complete the required safety trainings before you need them rather than after.

## Where you have to physically be

You lead and supervise, in person: every **light hang**, every **focus** session, every **dimmer check**, and **strike**. There is no version of this job that is run remotely, and "my assistant covered it" does not apply to those four.

Day to day through the whole production process, the hanging, cabling, troubleshooting and repair of lighting and electrical equipment is yours.

## The stuff people forget is yours

Special effects machines and their expendables. Tech and backstage systems that happen to be electrical: run lights, cue lights, Little Lites. Purchase and rental requests for anything the show needs and the inventory does not have, sometimes including the pickup and the return.

And at the end: a safe, efficient strike of all lighting and electrical gear, with the theatre and the storage areas left clean and organized. The condition you leave storage in is the condition the next HE inherits, and they will know it was you.
