# Deliverables

*What you owe, and roughly when. Source: URITP Head Electrician duties and responsibilities, version 2026. Content last checked: 2026-07-29.*

## Going in

You provide the Lighting Designer the paperwork they need to design against: plots, budget, inventory, and tech specs. Then you go the other direction and **track and review everything that comes back from the LD** with the Production Manager, the Technical Director, and the other department heads. You are the filter between a designer's drawing and what the building can actually do.

On every plot version, you provide a **feasibility update**. This is the most valuable thing you produce and the easiest to skip. It is also where you generate the parts the designer does not: the install plan and the cabling plan, which are yours to author, not theirs to submit.

**Before the process starts**, the lighting and cable inventories are accurate and up to date. Not "mostly". A budget conversation built on a wrong inventory count is a conversation you will have twice.

## Coming out

A **production archive** for each show, curated and submitted by you. What goes in it:

- the final plots, as hung, not as drawn
- the show file
- the patch list
- run sheets
- channel hookup, network and device arrangement, and tech notes

The distinction between as-hung and as-drawn is the entire point of the archive. A designer's file tells the next person what somebody wanted. Yours tells them what was actually in the air, which is the only version worth inheriting.

**After every production**, the lighting and cable inventories get updated again. Beginning and end, both, every time.

And you maintain the HE handbook itself. Which is what this package is, so if you are reading it and something in here is wrong, that is now your job.
