# Faculty appointments

*How the University employs and re-employs its non-tenure faculty, and what a review cycle asks of you. Read when a contract is coming up. Source: Appendix II, Contract-Based Faculty Guidelines, from Faculty Governance's Rules and Regulations. Content last checked 21 September 2026.*

The University runs several parallel appointment ladders, and the one you are on decides what your review measures. That is the whole reason these notes exist: the ladders sound interchangeable, they are not, and the difference shows up as which parts of your job the review is allowed to look at.

Everything here covers the School of Arts & Sciences and the Hajim School of Engineering & Applied Sciences. Theatre sits inside English, which sits inside Arts & Sciences, so it applies.

- **[The tracks](./the-tracks.md)** — the four appointment families, how a rank on one maps to a rank on another, and what conversion between them involves.
- **[Reappointment](./reappointment.md)** — the two review processes, who starts them and when, what you submit, and who reads it.

**Not written yet:** `promotion.md` — the promotion process is a separate machine from reappointment, with its own timeline, its own materials, and a requirement for three confidential internal letters. Appendix II Section III covers it and nobody has needed it here yet.

## Where the raw material is

The source document is Appendix II under Rules and Regulations on the Faculty Governance site, along with two referee-letter templates for the instructional-track promotion cases. A copy of all three is attached to the Contract Re-appointment task in ClickUp.

The University revises these guidelines. Anything read here should be checked against the live page before it is relied on in a submission, because a procedural rule that changed quietly is exactly the kind of thing that costs a deadline.

Your own appointment facts — dates, rank, track, what you submitted and when — are records rather than reference, so they live in ClickUp and not here. This package tells you how the system works; it does not know where you sit in it.
