# Reappointment

*The two review processes for contract-based faculty, the deadlines that govern them, what the candidate submits, and who reads it. Sources: Appendix II, Contract-Based Faculty Guidelines, Section II, and the University Faculty Handbook as updated January 2025. Content last checked 21 September 2026.*

Reappointment is the renewal of a contract-based faculty appointment at the same rank. It is a different machine from promotion, which moves you up a rank and is covered in Section III of the same appendix.

**Read this scope statement before using any deadline below.** Everything in this note comes from Appendix II Section II, and that section's ranks are the instructional ones: assistant, associate, and full professor of instruction. It does not describe a process for the teaching-track titles of lecturer, senior lecturer and full-time instructor, and neither does the Faculty Handbook. For a teaching-track appointment the governing instrument may be the offer letter itself, whose renewal language can differ substantially from what follows, so counting backwards from a contract end date using these deadlines can put a case months out of step in either direction. [The tracks](./the-tracks.md) covers that gap and what the Handbook does give regardless of track.

Where Section II does apply, there are two versions, a full review and a streamlined one, and which you get is not a matter of preference.

## Who starts it, and when

The chair or director initiates reappointment, in both versions. It is also their job to tell the candidate they are eligible and to make them aware that streamlined and full reviews both exist, which is a duty the guidelines state explicitly. Annual reviews from the chair are part of the same expectation.

The timeline runs backwards from the contract end date and it starts earlier than almost anyone expects.

The process is to be initiated at least twenty months before the contract ends. Materials are due to the Dean's office sixteen months before the contract ends. That gap exists so the Dean's office can review, then either build a contract renewal or issue written notice of non-renewal a full year before the contract ends, which the University Faculty Handbook requires.

The appendix works a concrete example for a contract ending 30 June 2027: the chair or director initiates on 1 November 2025, the case is due to the Dean's office by 15 March 2026, and notice of the renewal decision reaches the candidate by 30 June 2026. A year of runway, and roughly twenty months of process before that.

One thing to know about the one-year notice the schedule is built around: the Handbook grants it to an instructor, assistant professor, associate professor, or professor without tenure whose total service at those ranks exceeds two years, and on appointments of two years or less the notice period is five months instead. It also says plainly that failure to receive notification does not entitle anyone to reappointment, and the remedy for silence is to inform the dean or director, then the provost or president if no prompt response comes.

The practical consequence of a schedule that long is that the initiation deadline can pass without anybody noticing, and the person it costs is the candidate. Knowing your own contract end date and counting twenty months back from it is the cheapest protection available.

## Full review

The full review is used for the first reappointment, always. It can also be used for later ones, at the request of either the chair or director or the faculty member, so a candidate who wants the fuller reading can ask for it.

The candidate submits annual activity reports covering the whole current appointment, course evaluations covering the whole current appointment, and a current CV. Those first two are cumulative, which is the reason the annual report matters long before a review is on the horizon: the review reads what was filed at the time, and a year with no report filed is a gap in the record that cannot be backfilled convincingly.

The committee is three faculty from the home department or program, with at least two instructional-track voting members, and every member must hold rank above the person under review. An additional instructional-track faculty member at the candidate's own rank may sit in but cannot vote.

Small programs are common enough that the guidelines plan for them. If the department or program lacks enough eligible faculty, the chair or director may serve on the committee, or instructional-track faculty from outside the department who are familiar with the candidate's instructional work may serve instead.

And one provision belongs to the candidate rather than the administration: tenure-track faculty from the candidate's department may serve on the committee in place of instructional-track faculty if the candidate requests it. That is a right, exercisable by asking, and it changes who reads the file.

The committee writes a recommendation based on the submitted materials and its own understanding of the candidate's activities. The chair or director then reviews both the recommendation and the materials, and either states agreement or makes a different recommendation. Where reappointment is recommended, that statement is also where suggested duties for the next contract get proposed, so the chair's letter is not only a verdict on the past term, it is a draft of the next one. Everything then goes to the Dean's office.

## Streamlined review

The streamlined process is the short version, for an established faculty member with a consistently strong record. Three conditions have to hold: it is not the first reappointment, performance has been consistently strong, and the faculty member agrees to the streamlined route.

The first condition is absolute. The streamlined process may not be used for a first reappointment, which means a first-time candidate has no choice to make about which process to run.

When it does apply, the candidate submits a current CV and completes a streamlined attestation form that the Dean's office provides and that both candidate and chair or director sign. The chair or director adds a letter of support carrying proposed duties, and the courses taught with their enrollment counts and their connection to the curriculum.

Enrollment counts and curricular connection are worth noting as the evidence this version actually runs on. The streamlined case is largely a teaching-load argument.

## What the evidence has to cover

Both versions are read against the three pillars that define an instructional-track appointment: instructional work, service, and other engagement. The pillars are described at length in [the tracks](./the-tracks.md), and the reappointment guidance adds one emphasis of its own, that reappointment and promotion both look for evidence of good pedagogical practice, a commitment to delivering a strong student experience, and a reflective stance toward one's own teaching.

Reflection is named repeatedly and is easy to under-supply. It is not the same as reporting what was taught. A file that lists activity and never examines it satisfies the first pillar thinly and the reflective requirement not at all.

Where an appointment carries significant work that is not teaching, the pillar that holds it is service or other engagement, and it has to be documented to be read. Nothing in the process discovers unrecorded work. The annual activity report is the instrument for putting it on the record, year by year, while it is still fresh and verifiable.

## If a review is contested

Grievances about a reappointment decision are their own procedure in the Handbook, called Tenure and Appointment Grievances, and the grounds are narrow: procedural irregularities in how the decision was reached, or violation of academic freedom. Substance is not reviewable. Salary, teaching load and office space go through the separate General Grievance procedure, which explicitly may not be used for reappointment cases, so the two cannot be mixed and only one procedure may be used for any one grievance.

Which procedures are even available is keyed to Faculty Senate voting eligibility, and anyone can look their own eligibility up. Those who are not eligible use the HR grievance procedure instead. Where the grievant is contract-based, the January 2025 revisions require the nomination pool to include long-serving contract-based faculty, defined as having been through at least one reappointment with at least six years of service, and at least three of the five committee members must be contract-based.
